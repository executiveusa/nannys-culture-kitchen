# Brand Constitution — Nanny's Culture Kitchen

## Brand truth
Nanny's Culture Kitchen is a Puerto Vallarta plant-based culture kitchen named for the founder's mother, Nanny, from Washington, Louisiana.

## What we believe
Food can travel across cultures without turning culture into decoration.

## How we behave
- Feed people where they already are.
- Keep the food side plant-based.
- Let service shape the menu.
- Give guest cultures room to speak for themselves.
- Show what is real; label what is still a vision.

## What we refuse
- fabricated heritage
- theme-park New Orleans
- generic vegan branding
- fake farm claims
- fake operating proof
- trend-driven interface decoration

## What people should feel
Hungry, curious, looked after, and confident that the kitchen has a real point of view.

## What people should remember
A living tropical culture kitchen under a geodesic dome, with Louisiana roots in the name and food that comes to the worksite.

## What people should do
Request a worksite lunch.

## Governing idea
**A living kitchen: food, culture, growing, and service in the same frame.**

## Cultural behavior
- Puerto Vallarta is the environmental truth.
- Louisiana / New Orleans is a family and culinary influence, not the physical setting.
- Mardi Gras colors appear as small signals, not the full palette.
- Guest chefs/cultures must be credited and contextualized when that program becomes active.
